# Swarms: a crew of Pinchys on one big job

A swarm is a crew of equal Pinchys that takes one big job and works on it at the same time, deciding as they go on a shared board: each one takes the next useful piece, finishes it, puts it in and takes the next. Use it when a job is too big for one chat: building something with several pieces, a large refactor, research with many sources, an audit, a pile of files to process. For a small job a normal chat (or Code and Work mode) is cheaper and faster.

## What to ask for
- "Use a swarm to build a small command line tool in ~/code/notes that syncs my notes to a git repo, with tests and a README." He sets up the swarm and asks you once: "Start this swarm?" with the crew size, the cost cap and the folder. Say go and the crew gets to work at once; there is no plan to wait for. You can switch this question off in Settings; with the switch Actual full autonomy on everything (Settings → Experimental) on, it is not asked at all.
- "Here is the brief" with a file, a .zip or a whole folder attached: the crew gets them in its board folder and opens them before it starts.
- "Work through ~/Documents/update.md in ~/code/app" — a list of fixes and wishes for a project you already have. Every item goes on the board in your words, nothing lands until its tests pass, risky changes get a second lobster, and ideas that are not on your list come back to you as questions. New swarm has a card for it: "Work through an update list in my code".
- "Start a swarm to review ~/work/api for security problems. I want a findings list with file, line and severity." A crew reads the code in parallel and one part puts the list together.
- "How is the swarm doing?" He tells you which parts are done, who is working on what and what it has cost so far.
- "Tell the crew to use Postgres, not SQLite." The message goes on the swarm's board for everyone.
- "Ask Coral why the tests are slow." A private message to one member of the crew; only that one gets it.
- "Go wide over these 30 packages: which licence does each use?" Give a swarm a list and one question — pasted lines, a CSV with a header, or a list — and it answers the question for every row: up to six small agents work side by side, one row each at a time, under the swarm's one cost cap. The answers come back as one table (a .md, .csv and .json in the result). Name columns to get one column each (price, licence …). A row that keeps failing is marked failed instead of asking you; if the swarm stops early you still get the table, with the rows it didn't reach marked. In New swarm, the **Go wide over a list** chip does the same: your goal becomes the question, and a box takes the list (and optional columns).

You can also start one yourself: open the Workspace window, pick Swarm, then New swarm. There you write the goal, attach files or folders (paperclip or drop them), and choose a kind of job, a crew size (Auto is fine), a time limit, a cost cap, connectors and the folder for the results. **Start** starts the crew right away (this button is your go, nothing asks again). **Plan it first** runs a crew with a planner instead: one Pinchy writes the parts and you check and edit them before you press Start the crew.

## The swarm window
Open it from the Workspace window (Swarm tab) or by clicking a crew member on your desktop. It is laid out like a chat app:
- **Left: the conversations.** Board (the whole crew), Files (the board folder), a thread for each part that has a discussion, then the crew: one row per member with what it is doing right now. A member with nothing to do has a hollow dot and says "resting — nothing to do", "resting until "<part>" lands" or "thinks the goal is done". A row is also your private line to that member. "Between lobsters" lists what members wrote to each other in private; you can read every thread.
- **Middle: the conversation you picked**, with one message box under it. The small "To" label above the box says where your message goes: the Board, a part's thread, or one member only. Type @ and a name to get one member's attention on the board. Attach files or a whole folder with the paperclip, drop them on the box, or paste a screenshot; they are put in the board folder (a folder keeps its sub-folders) and the crew can open them. Code in a message has its own Copy button.
- **Ask about it** (top of the swarm window): a small side chat that answers what the crew is doing and why, from a snapshot of the swarm. Your question never reaches the crew and costs no crew turn.
- **Interrupt the crew**: a board message normally interrupts nobody (the crew reads it at their next step). Press "Interrupt the crew" before sending when everyone must read it now; it costs one turn per member.
- **Questions for you** are pinned above the message box wherever you are, with buttons when the asker offered choices. Each answer goes to that one question only.
- **Right: details.** Where the run is (Work · Check · Done for a hive; a crew with a plan also has Planning), the parts, the newest files, what it costs and what the crew carries. With a member selected it shows that member instead: its part, Pause, Restart, Remove, its model, and what it did. The button with the sliders at the top right shows or hides this pane; on a small window it starts hidden.
- **Top row:** the swarm's state, cost and time, Pause, and a menu (…) with Add workers, More time or money, Open the results folder and Stop the swarm. While the swarm works on your away server there is also **Pick up here**. A thin line across the top shows how many parts are done. The switch next to it changes the middle: Chat, Parts (the board of parts; click a part for who is on it, its proof and its changes), Files, Timeline, and Result once there is one. While a plan waits for your OK there is also Plan.
- **New swarm** shows example cards ("What a crew is good at"). Open one, fill in its fields and press "Use this": it fills in the goal, the folder and a crew size. Nothing starts until you press Start (or Plan it first).
- A finished swarm opens on its Result, with a box under it to ask for a follow-up round in the same folder.

## How it works
- By default a crew works as a **hive**: there is no planner and no plan to wait for. The first member puts the first pieces of the job on the board and starts one; the others join as soon as the first piece is on the board (after a minute and a half at the latest). Every member reads the board, **claims** the next useful piece nobody holds (or adds one it sees is missing), builds it, checks it and **lands** it, then takes the next one in the same go. Nobody waits for a teammate: a member that needs something from another asks it in a message and works on something else meanwhile.
- A member that sees nothing useful left for itself **rests**: it has no open session and costs nothing until something wakes it (a message, a new open piece, the piece it waits for landing). When everyone rests and nothing is open, the member who landed last checks the whole job against what you asked for, fixes or puts up what is missing, and then you get the result.
- **What you asked for**: when your goal is a list of asks (an update file, several wishes), the crew keeps them as its own list in the swarm window. An ask nobody is on yet says "Nobody on it yet" and becomes open work; at the end each ask says whether a teammate who didn't build it found it done.
- **Ideas for you**: an extra a member thinks of that you did not ask for is not built on its own — it waits in the swarm's Ideas list with **Build it** / **No**.
- Before the hand-over, a crew that landed two or more parts in its own copy (of a git project or of a normal folder) runs a **Code Review of its own whole change**; each confirmed problem (up to six) becomes a fix part for the crew, and the review's summary is in the result. It is skipped when less than $0.75 of the cost cap is left, and a crew working directly in your folder has no copy to review.
- A piece its member could not finish goes back on the board with a note for whoever can; the same piece stuck twice comes to you.
- Prefer the old way? Settings → Workspace → Swarms → How a crew works → "With a plan first": a planner reads the job and the folder and writes the parts first, and (if you want) you see that plan before any work starts.
- Each member of the crew works in its own copy of the folder, so nobody overwrites anybody. A finished part is merged into the swarm's shared result.
- A part is done when it is checked, not when its maker says so. Checks that are commands (tests, a build) are run by Pinchy himself. **Before a part lands, Pinchy runs the tests next to the files it changed** — a red test keeps it out, with the output, and a test that was already red before the part is asked about instead of quietly ignored. Parts that touch risky or shared files, are big, have no test, or change what you see get a **review by a teammate who didn't write them** (a change to the screen needs a picture); a failed review becomes a fix for its maker, and the result and each part's card say who checked what. With a plan first, a part Pinchy could not check himself is looked at by another member, who may improve it directly; you can change this to only the risky parts, every part or none.
- The crew has a board: a shared folder for anything they want to show each other (screenshots, drafts, outputs) and a board chat everyone can read. A message on the board does not interrupt anybody; when you write on it while the whole crew rests, the member that has rested longest wakes up to read it (one turn, not one per member).
- Private messages go to one member only. A member that is in the middle of work reads the message at its next step; it does not have to stop.
- When the crew needs you (a question, a choice, a part that is stuck) you get one question with buttons: in the swarm view, in the phone app, and in Telegram if you connected it. The others keep working on what they can.
- An agent can start its own sub-swarm for a job; it runs as a hive too, with the crew the agent picked (each member with its role), and its card shows in the agent's chat.
- A swarm that one of your agents started belongs to that agent. Its questions go to the agent first; what the agent cannot decide, or does not answer within 15 minutes, comes to you. More money and Apply always come to you, and so do permissions unless **Actual full autonomy on everything** is on (see Limits).
- If nothing can move without you, the swarm is parked: every session is closed, the clock stops, and waiting costs nothing. It carries on the moment you answer.
- At the end you get the result with its files. In a git project the changes wait on a branch until you press Apply; in a normal folder the files are put in place for you.
- A swarm that worked through a list of your asks (an update file with three or more things, for example) also writes a **release note** next to its result: what is new, in your words; what did not make it, with the reason; and how each change was checked. A line that only its builder checked is marked [CHECK] for you to confirm. It is a draft for you to read and edit — the swarm never publishes it.

## When Pinchy closes, restarts or updates
- An update waits while a swarm is working. A parked swarm does not hold an update back.
- If Pinchy is closed, crashes or restarts in the middle of a part, nothing is lost: each member continues its own conversation where it stopped instead of starting the part again.
- After an update or a crash the swarm carries on by itself. If you closed Pinchy yourself, he asks first. You can change this.
- A swarm you stopped yourself, one at its cost cap, or one that keeps getting cut off is never restarted without you. One that was cut off more than a week ago is not restarted at all (open it to carry on).

## Settings
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → How a crew works: "As a hive — they decide as they go" (the default) or "With a plan first — a planner splits the job into parts".
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → Show me the plan before a swarm I start gets to work: on by default. For a hive it is the one "Start this swarm?" question (no plan); with a plan first you check the parts. It applies to swarms you start from the chat; the Start button in New swarm is already your go, and swarms an agent, a task or the phone starts don't wait. With **Actual full autonomy on everything** (Experimental) on, nothing waits for this either; a plan you ask for yourself ("plan it first and show me") still does.
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → Models inside a swarm → "My rules": you pick the model for each kind of work under "Right model per piece" (planning and the final check, checking a teammate's work, hard parts, normal parts, tests, docs and notes, looking things up, short messages). Empty means the swarm's own model. Each part says which model it ran on (hover it for why). The helper jobs your agents start use the same rules. A member in the middle of a conversation is never moved to a weaker model, because restarting costs more than it saves.
- The crew reads a map of your project that Pinchy keeps from the files themselves (what each folder and file is, what it offers, how the project is checked). It costs nothing to make, stays on this computer (never in your project) and is refreshed every time it is used, so the crew stops re-reading your whole folder.
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms, the rest: how many agents a swarm may have and how many work at the same moment, how many swarms may run at once, which finished parts get a second look, where the agents work (own copy or the same folder), what each agent carries (tools and connectors), the models, the cost cap per swarm and the room in a swarm's board folder.
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → Carry on with a swarm that was cut off: after an update or a crash (ask me if I quit), always, ask me first, or never. The same setting decides whether a swarm that stopped at your plan's limit carries on by itself when the limit resets ("never" = it waits for you).
- "Move this swarm to my away server" — Workspace → the swarm → ⋯ → Move to away server. The crew stops for a moment and carries on there where it left off, each member in its own conversation; this computer can sleep or shut down. While it is away the swarm says "On your away server" with how far it got, and **Pick up here** brings it back with everything made there. Its result still waits for your Apply. A swarm that finished on the server comes back by itself when this computer wakes or starts, and you get a notice.
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → When this computer goes to sleep: keep swarms here (they pause), or move running swarms to your away server by themselves. With "move", Pinchy keeps the server's copy of the project up to date while the swarm works, so the move at sleep takes seconds; if you wake the computer before the move reached the server, it is called off and the swarm carries on here.
- Settings → Workspace → Swarms → Show swarm helpers on screen as little Pinchys: the crew walking around on your desktop while they work.
- A project can have its own rules in a file `.pinchy/swarm.json` (which commands check a part, the final check, heavy jobs, house rules for the crew). Pinchy shows you what the file asks for and uses it only after you approved it; if the file changes later, he asks again. With **Actual full autonomy on everything** on, the file is used as it is for that run and the swarm chat says so once (it is not stored as approved: with the switch off he asks again).

## Limits
- A swarm uses your Claude plan much faster than one chat: every member is its own Claude session. The cost cap per swarm (20 US dollars of usage by default) stops it when reached; you can resume it with a higher cap.
- When your plan's usage is nearly used up (95 %), or when the swarm's time runs out, nothing new starts but the members who are in the middle of something finish it first, so no half-done work is thrown away. A swarm stopped by the plan limit says when the limit resets and carries on by itself then, with the time it had left. One stopped by its time can go on when you give it more time (also while it is finishing up).
- Moving a swarm to the away server needs a swarm that works in its own copy (the normal way); one that works directly in your folder cannot move. Dependency folders like node_modules are not copied to the server.
- How many members work at the same moment depends on your computer's free memory. On a small machine they take turns; a member that rests frees its place for one with work.
- Files you give a swarm: up to 10 files or folders per message (and with the goal); a folder brings up to 300 of its files, at most 8 folders deep (.git, .hg, .svn, node_modules, __pycache__ and links are skipped); one file up to 200 MB; the whole board folder up to its room in Settings (2000 MB by default).
- Builds, emulators and other heavy jobs from all swarms wait in one line so they do not exhaust the computer's memory.
- With the default toolbox ("Focused") the crew works on files, commands and web pages for the job. It does not use your mouse and keyboard, your wallet or admin (sudo) commands, and it cannot change Pinchy's settings.
- Anything that needs your OK still comes to you; the crew cannot approve it for itself. The exception is the one you switch on yourself: the switch **Actual full autonomy on everything** in Settings → Experimental. While it is on, nothing in a swarm asks you (no start question, no plan check, no permission) except a sudo command, which keeps its password dialog; the cost cap still stops the swarm, and the push / publish rule below still holds.
- A swarm never pushes, publishes or opens a pull request: the crew's commands for git push, gh pr / release, npm / PyPI publish and Pinchy's own release scripts are refused in every permission mode. What it made waits for your Apply; anything that leaves your computer is your step.
