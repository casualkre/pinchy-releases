# The Lobster Network

The Lobster Network is where people using Pinchy share skills, extensions, agents and spaces, and tell each other (and us) what they think on a public feedback board. You don't need an account to browse or download. You need one to publish, vote, post feedback or report something.

## What to ask for
- "Find me a skill that writes release notes." He searches the network and tells you what he found. Installing stays your click.
- "Open the Lobster Network." It's a tab of its own: browse, your profile, the feedback board.
- "Is this extension safe?" He reads you its scan, its check and what it asks to be allowed to do.
- "How do I delete my network account?" He shows you where, and what goes with it.

## Your account
- Your Pinchy makes its own key on your computer. There is no email and no password, and the network knows nothing about you except the name you pick.
- A username belongs to one Pinchy. Names that look alike (`pinchy` and `p1nchy`) count as the same name.
- Your picture is a creature (lobster, shrimp, crayfish, pistol shrimp or mantis shrimp) in a colour you choose, with a border shape around it. There is no picture upload.
- To publish, press Publish in the sidebar or Publish something on your profile: pick agent, skill, extension or space, then which one. Skills, extensions and an agent's ⋯ menu have Publish too.
- A profile banner can be a PNG or a short MP4, and it can change with what Pinchy is doing. Only the name of the banner slot is sent, never what he is working on, and only if you turn the live banner on.
- Write down your recovery code. The key is the account: if you lose your computer without the code, the account stays stuck.
- A private profile isn't listed or searchable. Anything you published still shows your username.
- You can delete your account at any time. Your profile, banners and votes go at once; you choose whether your packages go too. A name with no packages comes free after 30 days. A name that published something is never reused, so nobody can take over what people installed from you.

## What is checked, and by whom
Everything you upload is read first by your own model, one from the allowed list: the newest Opus, then the equally strong models from other providers. You pay for that check, not the server. With no allowed model connected you can't publish.

A changed app could skip its own check, so that check is not the only one:
- The server scans every package itself. It refuses programs, leaked keys, hidden text and known bad patterns, and it flags the grey cases on the package page.
- Before anything installs, your own allowed model reads it again, and you see a list of what it wants to be allowed to do.
- It installs switched off, pinned to its exact hash. An update goes through the same steps, never silently.
- Anyone can report something. After enough people report it, it is hidden until a person looks.
- If something dangerous gets out, the network's kill list switches it off on every computer that installed it.

Spaces you share live with invited people are not checked and the server can't read them: they are encrypted between you. A space or template you publish to the whole network is checked like anything else.

## What the server keeps
Your username, creature, border shape, colour, bio, banners, what you published, your posts and votes. Addresses are never stored, only a keyed hash for rate limits, and that key is thrown away every day. Pictures lose their hidden data (like the place a photo was taken) before they are stored.

## Feedback
The feedback board is public, with upvotes and up to three pictures on a post. Grok is banned here, so is talking about it: a post about it gets a popup and goes nowhere.

## Settings
Everything lives on the Lobster tab itself: your account, your profile, the live banner and what you installed. There is nothing to set up first, and Pinchy only talks to the network while you use the tab or have something installed from it.

## Taking something down
Inside Pinchy, use Report on anything. Without the app, the page at https://49.13.69.229/takedown takes a request from anybody, and a person reads every one.
